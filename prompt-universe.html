What is a prompt?
Beginner
▼

A prompt is the instruction you give an AI. Think of it like giving directions to a taxi driver — vague directions get you lost, specific ones get you exactly where you want to go.

Weak prompt → generic answer. Strong prompt → exactly what you need.

Weak
Write me a blog post about AI.
Strong
You are a technology journalist writing for Wired. CONTEXT: Audience is non-technical startup founders, age 30-45. TASK: Write a 700-word article explaining how AI agents will change product development in 2026. Cover: (1) what agents are, (2) three real startup use-cases, (3) what founders should do now. FORMAT: No jargon, H2 headers, punchy 2-3 sentence paragraphs. CONSTRAINTS: No hype language, no "revolutionary", cite a real example per section.
🏗️
The 5-Block Formula
Core skill
▼

Every great prompt has up to 5 blocks. You don't need all five — but the more you fill, the better the output.

01 ROLE — Who should the AI be? "You are a senior UX researcher at Figma..."
02 CONTEXT — What background does it need? "My app has 12k daily users, React frontend, 40% mobile..."
03 TASK ★ — What exactly do you need? "Analyse the 3 biggest usability issues. Number each one..."
04 FORMAT — How should output look? "Bullet points, 500 words max, Grade 10 reading level..."
05 CONSTRAINTS — What should it NOT do? "No 'it depends' answers, no invented stats, avoid jargon..."

6 Power Techniques
Intermediate
▼

These techniques can be toggled in the Builder. Here's what each one does:

🧠 Chain-of-ThoughtAsks AI to reason step-by-step before answering. Best for complex tasks, analysis, or math.
🎯 Few-ShotGive 2 examples of what you want. AI matches your exact style and tone.
🚫 Negative PromptingExplicitly list what to avoid. "No bullet points" works better than "write in prose."
📐 Force JSONMakes AI return structured data. Perfect when you'll use the output in code.
🔄 5 VariantsAI gives 5 different angles first. You pick which to develop. Saves revision rounds.
🔍 Self-CheckAI reviews its own answer before showing you. Catches vague sections and missed requirements.

The #1 Mistake Beginners Make
Must-read
▼

Being too vague. The AI is not a mind-reader. If you'd be embarrassed to hand your prompt to a human colleague as a brief, it's too vague.

The fix: specificity beats length. You don't need 2000 words — you need precise, concrete language.

Too vague
"Write about marketing" "Make it professional" "Be an expert" "Don't make it long"
Specific
"Write about B2B email marketing" "800 words, H2 headers, no jargon" "Senior copywriter at HubSpot" "Maximum 600 words"

Prompting for different AI tools
Advanced
▼

Different AI tools need different strategies. The builder adapts hints per category:

💬 Chatbots (Claude, GPT)Use Role + numbered Task + Constraints. Chain-of-thought works great here.
🖼️ Image Gen (Midjourney)Lead with subject, then lighting, then style reference. Use "--ar" for ratio.
🎬 Video AI (Sora, Kling)Describe camera movement separately from subject action. Specify crowd size exactly.
💻 Coding (Copilot, Cursor)Specify your full stack + versions. Say "complete file, not a diff." Number requirements.
🎵 Audio AI (Suno, Udio)Use "X meets Y" with real artist names. Always include BPM, key, and duration.
🔍 SEO contentInclude keyword difficulty + your DA + competitor DAs. Ask for realistic angle.
